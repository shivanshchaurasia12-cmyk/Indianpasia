Leverage in Trading: What a 1% Move Does to a ₹20,000 Account

2 min read · Educational only

Leverage lets you control a big position with a small deposit. The deposit is called margin. A lot is the standard size of one trade, and a pip is the smallest usual price step in currency pairs.

The catch: profit and loss are calculated on the full position, not on your margin.

Worked example

Your account has ₹20,000. The price moves 1% against you. Here is the loss at different leverage levels:

LeveragePosition sizeLoss on a 1% move% of your account
None (1x)₹20,000₹2001%
10x₹2,00,000₹2,00010%
50x₹10,00,000₹10,00050%
100x₹20,00,000₹20,000100% (wiped out)

What to take from this

  • Margin needed = position size ÷ leverage.
  • Higher leverage means a smaller price move can end your account.
  • Fix the amount you can lose on one trade (for example 1-2% of the account) before you pick a position size.
